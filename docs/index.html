Welcome. WT Session Readout turns the match logs you copy out of War Thunder into clear numbers: win rate, Silver Lions (SL), Research Points (RP), what you are researching, and how many matches a goal will take. Everything runs inside your web browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

This is the Development version of the site. It is where new things are tried before they reach the stable release, so things can change. The documentation describes how this version works today.

Getting started

This page takes you from nothing to your first results in about two minutes.

1. Open the site

Open the hosted page in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari — desktop or phone). There is nothing to install and no account to make.

The first time you open each view, a short guided tour appears. It is skippable (Skip tour, or the Esc key) and can be reopened any time from ? Tutorial. See The guided tours.

In War Thunder, open your battle results history and copy the text of one or more matches. Each match report starts with a line such as Victory in the [Mode] … mission! (or Defeat…) and ends with the Session and Total lines. You can copy many matches at once. Reading your logs shows what to look for.

No logs handy? Click Load Example Data to try the tool with a sample session.

  1. Click the big text box and paste.
  2. Click Analyze.

Key concepts

This page explains the ideas behind the numbers, so every screen makes sense. It describes what the site does with your logs; for game rules themselves, the game is the authority.

The match is the unit

Everything starts with one match report — the text War Thunder shows for a finished battle. The site reads it into a small record: result (win or loss), battle type, mission, net SL, RP, research targets, time played and a Session ID. All totals and averages are built by adding these records up.

Two kinds of currency

  • SL (Silver Lions) — money. The site shows net SL: what you earned minus what the match cost (repairs, ammunition and crew replenishment, respawns). A match can have a high earned SL and a much lower net SL.
  • RP (Research Points) — what you spend to unlock vehicles and modules. The site counts a match's CRP as its RP (see Reading your logs).

Where RP goes: new vehicle vs module/progress

A match can earn RP toward more than one thing at once. The site keeps them apart:

BucketWhere you see itMeaning
Research targetRP by research targetRP that went toward unlocking a whole new vehicle
Other RP (module / progress)Other RP (collapsed)RP that went toward modules and research progress on a vehicle

They are not added together, because the same match performance is credited to both. Seeing a big "Other RP" number does not mean you earned extra — it is a different view of the same earning.

Net SL vs earned SL

The Advanced view's match detail shows both. Earned SL is what the match paid out; net SL subtracts costs. The totals on the Basic view use net SL, because it reflects what you actually gained.

Wins, losses and averages

A match is a win (Victory) or a loss (Defeat). The site averages SL and RP separately for wins and for losses because they are very different. Those four averages (plus your win rate) drive the goal calculator: your expected income per match is the win rate times the average win plus the loss rate times the average loss.

Battle types (categories)

Different modes pay very differently, so mixing them hides the truth. The site splits matches into battle types by looking at the mode name. You can define your own groups (Battle-type categories).

Sessions, duplicates and the Session ID

Each match report carries a unique Session ID. It lets the site recognise a match it has already seen:

  • while analyzing, repeats are struck through and left out of every total;
  • in the archive, only one copy per Session ID is ever kept.

A "session" in this site's language is simply what is in the paste box plus anything you imported — not a game session.

Detail vs summary

  • A match with detail (●) has its original text stored. The Advanced view can unpack it into vehicles, events, costs and research.
  • A match with only a summary (○) was imported from a minimal export or HTML report. It still counts in totals, maps and win rates, but has no breakdown.

Raw exports keep the original text, so they are the only backup that preserves detail.

Everything is local

Parsing, counting, compression, storage and colour handling all happen in your browser. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere. See Privacy and safety.

Reading your logs

The site analyzes the text that War Thunder gives you for each finished match. You do not have to clean it up — paste it as it is, several matches at a time if you like.

What a match looks like

A match report contains, in order:

  1. A headline — Victory or Defeat in the battle mode, naming the mission (for example Victory in the [Mode] Mission name mission!).
  2. The events of the match — your scoring events (hits, kills, assists, awards, and so on) with the Silver Lions (SL) and Research Points (RP) each one earned.
  3. Rewards and costs — flat rewards, and the repair, ammunition and respawn costs.
  4. Research — which vehicle you were researching (a whole new vehicle unlocked) and which module or research progress received RP.
  5. Used items — consumables such as boosters, when listed.
  6. The Session line — a unique code identifying that match.
  7. The Total line — net SL, CRP and RP for the match.

Where the numbers come from

NumberMeaning
Net SLSilver Lions earned in the match after costs, taken from the Total line.
RP (what the site counts)The match's CRP figure from the Total line.
Research targetsRP that went toward unlocking a whole new vehicle ("Researched unit").
Other RPRP that went to modules or research progress on a different vehicle ("Researching progress"). Kept apart so the two are never mixed.
Win / lossFrom the headline, Victory or Defeat.
Time playedFrom the match's own time information, used for the goal calculator's time estimate.

Why CRP and not the last "RP" figure?

The Total line can end with a third figure labelled RP. That figure is CRP plus the research-progress RP added together — the same match performance counted toward two things at once, not an extra payout. Using it would inflate every average and the goal calculator's estimates. The site therefore uses CRP as a match's RP.

Session IDs and duplicates

Every match has a unique Session ID. The site uses it to avoid counting a match twice:

  • Pasting overlapping ranges of your history is fine — a match seen again is struck through and left out of every total.
  • The archive keeps one copy per Session ID.
  • A match with no readable Session ID cannot be recognised as a repeat, so each such paste counts separately. If you see this, paste each match once.

What if text looks different?

Game text can change between War Thunder updates. If a paste is "not recognised", make sure it includes the headline (Victory/Defeat in the …) and the Total line. Lines that are not understood are skipped rather than breaking the analysis. See Troubleshooting.

Battle types (categories)

Each match is placed in a battle type by looking at its mode name. By default anything containing Tank Assault is its own category, and everything else is Random Battles. You can add your own rules — see The Basic view.

Goal calculator

In-depth guide with worked arithmetic: The goal calculator in depth.

Estimates how many matches you need to reach a goal, using your own averages.

  1. Load averages from — choose a battle type (or All Combined). Win rate, average win/loss RP and average win/loss SL are filled in from your data. You can edit any of them to ask "what if".
  2. RP goal remaining and SL goal remaining — type what you still need (either or both).
  3. +30% RP Booster — toggle if you use the booster; it multiplies expected RP by 1.3.

You get: expected RP and net SL per match, expected time per match, matches needed for each goal, and an estimated playtime. The big number is matches needed for whichever goal takes longer, labelled RP-bound, SL-bound or tied. Leave a goal empty to ignore it.

Tip: averages come from your own matches, so the estimate gets better with more data and with the right battle type chosen.

The goal calculator in depth

The goal calculator answers one question: "At the rate I actually play, how many more matches until I reach this goal?" It uses your own averages, so it gets more useful the more matches you have analyzed.

The inputs

FieldWhat it isWhere the starting value comes from
Load averages fromWhich battle type's averages to useA drop-down of your categories plus All Combined
Win rate %Share of matches you winThe chosen category's win rate
Avg win RP / Avg loss RPAverage RP in a win / in a lossThe chosen category
Avg win SL / Avg loss SLAverage net SL in a win / in a lossThe chosen category
RP goal remainingRP you still needYou type it
SL goal remainingSL you still needYou type it
+30% RP BoosterToggle: multiply expected RP by 1.3You choose

Every field can be edited after loading. Changing one updates the answer immediately — that is how you ask "what if my win rate were 70%?".

The arithmetic

Let w be the win rate as a fraction (62.7% → 0.627).

  • Expected RP per match = (w × avg win RP + (1 − w) × avg loss RP), times 1.3 if the booster is on.
  • Expected net SL per match = w × avg win SL + (1 − w) × avg loss SL.
  • Expected time per match = w × average win time + (1 − w) × average loss time.
  • Matches needed — RP goal = RP goal ÷ expected RP, rounded up.
  • Matches needed — SL goal = SL goal ÷ expected net SL, rounded up.
  • The headline matches needed is the larger of the two (you must satisfy both). It is labelled RP-bound, SL-bound or tied.
  • Estimated playtime = matches needed × expected time per match.

Worked example (the sample session, All Combined)

Win rate 62.7%, avg win RP 5,769, avg loss RP 2,468, avg win SL 26,547, avg loss SL 10,033.

  • Expected RP = 0.627 × 5,769 + 0.373 × 2,468 ≈ 4,538
  • Expected net SL = 0.627 × 26,547 + 0.373 × 10,033 ≈ 20,387
  • Goals 60,000 RP and 300,000 SL: 60,000 ÷ 4,538 = 13.2 → 14; 300,000 ÷ 20,387 = 14.7 → 15
  • Headline: 15 matches, SL-bound; at 14:26 per match, about 3.6 hours.

With the booster: expected RP = 4,538 × 1.3 ≈ 5,899 → 60,000 ÷ 5,899 = 10.2 → 11 RP matches. Headline still 15 (SL).

Reading the result

  • Only one goal filled in? Leave the other empty. The empty one is ignored and the label follows the one you set.
  • A dash (—) means there isn't enough to compute (no goal entered, or the expected value is zero).
  • "Tied" means both goals take the same number of matches.
  • Expected time needs time data; if your matches have none, time shows as a dash.

Choosing the right averages

  • Pick the battle type you will actually play. In the sample, Random Battles and Tank Assault differ hugely; "All Combined" blends them and describes neither.
  • Very few matches in a category means shaky averages. Three matches is an anecdote, not a rate. Edit the fields by hand if you know better.
  • The booster is only a toggle for RP. It does not touch SL or time.
  • Vehicle-specific play. If a vehicle earns very differently from the rest, analyze only logs from it (or filter in the Advanced view) and type the averages in by hand.

Honest limits

  • It is an expectation, not a promise: real streaks happen.
  • It assumes future matches look like past ones — same mode, same vehicles, same habits.
  • It does not know about game events, premium status changes, or reward changes between updates.
  • Averages are of net SL (after costs), so an expensive-to-repair vehicle already lowers them.
  • RP uses CRP per match (why).

Questions people ask

Why does my RP goal say 14 but the headline says 15? The headline is whichever goal takes longer; here SL does.

Can I get "hours" for a goal in a particular mode? Choose that mode in Load averages from; the time estimate uses that mode's average win and loss time.

Is my goal saved? Not between visits; the loaded averages follow your data and the goal fields start empty each time.

Battle-type categories in depth

Different game modes pay differently, so the site groups matches into battle types and shows each group's numbers separately. The rules that decide the group are yours to edit.

How a match gets its category

  1. The site reads the mode name from the match headline (the part in square brackets).
  2. It walks your rules from top to bottom. A rule is a keyword and a label.
  3. The first rule whose keyword appears anywhere in the mode name wins. The comparison ignores upper/lower case, and the keyword only has to be contained in the mode name (it doesn't need to match the whole thing).
  4. If no rule matches, the match is a Random Battles match.

So a rule with the keyword Assault would catch Tank Assault, Air Assault, or anything else containing the word.

The default rules

A fresh install has one rule: Tank Assault → Tank Assault. Everything else is Random Battles.

Adding a rule

  1. Open Battle-type categories on the Basic view.
  2. Type a keyword you can see in a mode name (for example Arcade).
  3. Type the label you want to see in tables (for example Arcade Battles).
  4. Click Add rule.
  5. Click Analyze again to re-sort your matches.

Rules are saved in your browser as you type. Every rule row can be edited in place, and Remove deletes it.

Tips for good keywords

  • Use a distinctive word from the mode name, not a whole sentence.
  • Check the exact spelling in your logs: the Per-match detail table shows each match's mode.
  • Keep the label short; it appears in the split table, the goal calculator's drop-down and the Advanced view's filter.
  • Two rules may share one label — handy to merge two modes into one group.

Order matters

Rules are checked in the order shown, and the first hit wins. If one keyword is contained in another (say Assault and Tank Assault), the earlier rule takes the match. There is no drag-to-reorder: remove and re-add a rule to change its position.

What changes when you edit a rule

  • Basic view: the next Analyze uses the new rules.
  • Advanced view: matches are re-classified when the page is opened or refreshed, using your current rules; the Battle type filter shows the labels in use.
  • Saved archive: your matches are not changed on disk; the category is decided when they are shown.
  • Exports: an HTML report or minimal export records the categories as they were when you exported.

Limits

  • Up to 100 rules are kept.
  • Keywords and labels are cut at 100 characters; text is always displayed safely as plain text.
  • Rules live in your browser only and are not part of a raw export — if you move to another device, re-create them (they are short).

Phone, offline and slow devices

The site is built to be reliable on small screens, old devices and poor connections. This page explains what you will see and what you can do.

On a phone

Below about 680 pixels wide the top bar's controls (view switch, tutorial, GitHub link, colours) would not fit in a row, so they fold behind a Menu button.

  • Tap Menu to open the controls. The same menu lists Jump to links for the page's sections (for example Overall, Goal calculator, Per-match detail on the Basic view, or Library, Insights, Storage on the Advanced view). A section is only listed while it is on screen.
  • Tap outside the menu, press Esc, or pick an item to close it.
  • The guided tours open the menu for you when a step points at something inside it.
  • Wide screens keep the normal top bar.

Tables scroll sideways when they are wider than the screen. Tip: the Text size setting (Colours and text) can make everything easier to read.

On a slow connection

  • The whole site is small: about 60 KB of data for a first visit, and it is loaded in a handful of files rather than dozens, so there are fewer slow round trips.
  • If loading takes longer than a few seconds you will see a calm note at the bottom: Still loading — this is taking a while on your connection. The page will work in a moment. It disappears by itself.
  • If part of the page fails to arrive, a note says Part of this page didn't load — check your connection with a Reload button, instead of leaving a page that looks fine but does nothing.
  • The optional parts (colour panel, tutorials, phone menu) load after the main page, so you can start pasting and analyzing sooner.
  • Your saved colour theme is applied from the very first moment even on a slow connection.

Offline use

After your first successful visit the site saves itself in your browser (a "service worker" cache), so:

  • return visits open instantly, even on a terrible connection;
  • the whole app works with no connection at all — analyzing, the archive, the Advanced view and insights all work offline;
  • the example data works offline once it has been loaded at least once.

Updates

When a new version is published it is downloaded quietly in the background. It never swaps in by itself while you are working. A note appears — A new version is ready. — with Reload and Later.

  • Reload switches to the new version.
  • Later dismisses the note; it is offered again next visit.

If you close every tab of the site, the new version simply takes over next time you open it.

Offline limits

  • Load Example Data needs a connection the first time.
  • The very first visit needs a connection (there is nothing saved yet).
  • Opening the page directly from a file on disk skips the offline cache.
  • This documentation is not part of the offline cache.

Developers on localhost

The offline cache is off when the site is opened from localhost, so edits are never hidden behind a cache. See How it works for how to turn it on for testing.

Big sessions

With thousands of matches the page stays responsive:

  • The Basic view's match table shows the first 200 rows with Show 500 more / Show all.

  • The Advanced view lists matches without unpacking them, and fills its insight tables from small stored summaries.

Very large pasted logs (megabytes of text) can still make colour changes and the colour panel feel slower on weak devices, because the browser has to lay out all that text in the paste box. If that happens: Clear the box after analyzing (your archive keeps the matches).

Tips and tricks

Pasting

  • Paste a lot at once. There is no need to do one match at a time; paste a whole stretch of history.
  • Overlap is safe. Pasting the same matches again never double-counts them — repeats are matched by Session ID.
  • Paste each match once if it has no Session ID. Matches without a readable ID can't be recognised as repeats.
  • Paste, Analyze, Clear, repeat. The archive keeps every match you analyze, so you can clear the box between sessions without losing history.

Getting better numbers

  • Add rules for your game modes. Use the Battle-type categories section so Arcade, events or anything else gets its own row and its own averages for the goal calculator.
  • Pick the right battle type in the goal calculator. The averages it loads come from the type you choose; "All Combined" mixes everything.
  • Play with "what if". Edit the win rate or the average RP/SL in the goal calculator to see how a better win rate changes the number of matches needed.
  • Use the booster toggle if you play with the +30% RP booster so the estimate matches reality.
  • RP vs "Other RP". "RP by research target" is what goes toward new vehicles; module and research-progress RP is in the collapsed "Other RP" table. Both matter; they just aren't mixed.

Advanced view

  • Hover amounts in a match's detail to see the base, premium-account and booster parts of every SL and RP figure.
  • Use "Detail only" to hide summary-only matches when you want vehicle or event analysis.
  • Open the insight tables you need. Vehicle and event tables fill in when opened.
  • Sort by "Longest" or "Most SL" to find your outliers quickly.
  • Export just one match from its detail panel to share or save it.

Backups

  • Export Raw before you clear browser data, switch phones or reinstall your browser.
  • Prefer .json.gz — it's the smallest, and the same data.
  • Check the match count in the Advanced view's Storage section after a restore.

Looks

  • Try Daylight in bright sunlight or before printing.
  • Raise the text size on a small screen instead of zooming the whole browser.
  • Move a setup between devices: export your colour presets and import them on the other device.
  • Change two colours in a row to go from a dark to a light look; the site waits until the pair is readable.

Speed

  • Clear the paste box after analyzing a very large log (the archive keeps the matches).
  • Use "Show all" in the Basic table only when you need to scroll everything; the first 200 rows are usually enough.
  • Visit once while online: after that, every later visit is instant and works offline (Phone, offline and slow devices).

  • Esc closes the tour, the Customise panel, the phone menu and the match-detail panel.
  • ← → move through the tour steps.
  • Buttons, swatches and table rows can all be reached with Tab and activated with Enter or Space.

Security practices

How the project protects you, why, and what is left. The guiding promise: nothing leaves your device unless you export it yourself (Project rules).

The practices below follow widely used guidance, mainly from the OWASP Cheat Sheet Series (Content Security Policy, DOM-based XSS prevention, HTML5 security) and general GitHub repository hardening advice.

What is in place

1. Nothing can phone home

  • A strict Content-Security-Policy on every page denies everything by default and allows scripts, styles, images, connections and workers only from the site itself. Fonts, media, frames, plugins and manifests are denied outright. Even a mistake in the code could not send data to another address — the browser would refuse.
  • No form can submit anywhere, and the page's base address can't be changed.
  • The pages tell the browser never to send a Referer header, and the one external link (GitHub) opens with no access back to the page.
  • Automated tests use both views for real while recording every request, fetch, beacon and socket, and scan the source for anything capable of reaching another site. See Project rules.

2. Untrusted text stays text

Everything from a log, a file or saved settings is escaped or built as plain text before it is shown. This follows OWASP's advice to prefer safe ways of putting text on a page. Hostile test logs (scripts, images, odd names) are part of the test suite.

3. Imports are rebuilt, not trusted

Each imported match is re-created from validated fields; bad entries are skipped and counted. Size limits protect against oversized files and "zip bomb" archives; parsing patterns are bounded so crafted text can't stall the page. The limits are listed in Privacy and safety.

4. No third-party code

There are no libraries, frameworks, CDNs, trackers or fonts from elsewhere. That removes a whole class of supply-chain risk. The only dependency is the browser itself. (Test tooling is separate and never shipped.)

5. No powerful permissions

The site never asks for camera, microphone, location, notifications or similar, and never uses cookies. A test fails if code for those appears.

6. A careful offline cache

The service worker (offline copy) only handles same-origin GET requests inside its own folder, stores only the site's own files under a version name, only deletes caches carrying its own prefix, and never swaps versions while you work — you press Reload. A failed install leaves nothing behind. This follows OWASP's service-worker advice to restrict scope and keep it same-origin.

7. Exports are inert

An exported HTML report carries its own strict policy (nothing external, one nonce-tagged script) and no Referer. Exports contain only what you already see.

How it works

A short, plain-language tour of what is under the hood. You don't need any of this to use the site.

The big picture

The site is a set of static web pages. Your browser downloads them once and runs everything locally. There is no server doing the analysis.

  1. Reading. Pasted text is split into matches. Each match's headline, scoring events, costs, research lines, Session ID and Total line are read with careful, bounded patterns (so strange text can't make them run away).
  2. Checking. Every value is cleaned and limited before it is used.
  3. Counting. Duplicates (same Session ID) are set aside; the rest are totalled by battle type, research target and so on.
  4. Saving. The original text of each match is compressed and stored in your browser's database, along with a small summary used for fast listing and insights.
  5. Showing. Tables are built with all text escaped.

The two views

  • Basic works on what you paste (plus anything you import).
  • Advanced works on the saved archive. It lists matches from the stored summaries without unpacking anything, and only decompresses a match when you open it.

The archive

Each match is stored by its Session ID, so the same match can only exist once. Compression uses the browser's built-in gzip, done on a background worker so the page doesn't freeze. Insight tables use per-match rollups saved with each match; if the rollup format ever improves, old ones are rebuilt automatically. Summaries carry a version for the same reason.

Colours

Colours are stored as nine values and applied to the page through CSS variables. A tiny script in the page head applies your saved look before the first paint, which is why there's no flash of the default theme. Readability is measured with standard contrast ratios.

Speed and reliability features

  • Bundled files. The pages load one core file each (plus an optional "extras" file), instead of ~15 separate ones.
  • Load guard. A tiny script shows still loading or part of this page didn't load notes with a Reload button.
  • Background worker. Compression and insight rollups run off the main thread, with a fallback if workers aren't available.
  • Service worker (offline cache). After the first visit the whole app is cached under a version name. Updates wait for you to press Reload. It only ever touches caches that start with its own prefix. On localhost it's off unless you set a browser setting called enableServiceWorkerLocally to true.
  • Capped tables. The Basic match table shows 200 rows at a time; the Advanced one 500.

For people who change the site

The scripts the pages load are generated from the source files by a small build step, and tests rebuild them automatically and fail if they're stale. The project README explains the workflow, and the test suite README explains the checks (parsing, both views, storage, security, colours, theme flash, offline, build, phone menu, size-agnostic invariants and performance). The low-end investigation is in tests/results/low-end-report.md.
