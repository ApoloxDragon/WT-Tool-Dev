WT Session Readout — Documentation

Welcome. WT Session Readout turns the match logs you copy out of War Thunder into clear numbers: win rate, Silver Lions (SL), Research Points (RP), what you are researching, and how many matches a goal will take. Everything runs inside your web browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

This is the Development version of the site. It is where new things are tried before they reach the stable release, so things can change. The documentation describes how this version works today.

Getting started

This page takes you from nothing to your first results in about two minutes.

1. Open the site

Open the hosted page in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari — desktop or phone). There is nothing to install and no account to make.

The first time you open each view, a short guided tour appears. It is skippable (Skip tour, or the Esc key) and can be reopened any time from ? Tutorial. See The guided tours.

2. Get some log text

In War Thunder, open your battle results history and copy the text of one or more matches. Each match report starts with a line such as Victory in the [Mode] … mission! (or Defeat…) and ends with the Session and Total lines. You can copy many matches at once. Reading your logs shows what to look for.

No logs handy? Click Load Example Data to try the tool with a sample session.

3. Paste and Analyze

  1. Click the big text box and paste.
  2. Click Analyze.

The results appear under the box: overall totals, a split by battle type, the goal calculator, RP per research target and a table of every match.

4. What happens to your matches

Every match you analyze is also saved, compressed, in your browser (a small note under the buttons tells you how many are archived). That saved archive is what the Advanced view reads. It stays on this device, in this browser only.

Important: because the archive lives only in your browser, clearing your browser's site data deletes it. Use Export Raw now and then as a backup — see Backups, import and export.

5. Next steps

  • Try the goal calculator to see how many matches a research goal needs: The Basic view.
  • Click Advanced view → to open a single match in detail.
  • Pick a colour theme with the swatches in the top bar.
  • Read Tips and tricks.

Reading your logs

The site analyzes the text that War Thunder gives you for each finished match. You do not have to clean it up — paste it as it is, several matches at a time if you like.

What a match looks like

A match report contains, in order:

  1. A headline — Victory or Defeat in the battle mode, naming the mission (for example Victory in the [Mode] Mission name mission!).
  2. The events of the match — your scoring events (hits, kills, assists, awards, and so on) with the Silver Lions (SL) and Research Points (RP) each one earned.
  3. Rewards and costs — flat rewards, and the repair, ammunition and respawn costs.
  4. Research — which vehicle you were researching (a whole new vehicle unlocked) and which module or research progress received RP.
  5. Used items — consumables such as boosters, when listed.
  6. The Session line — a unique code identifying that match.
  7. The Total line — net SL, CRP and RP for the match.

Where the numbers come from

NumberMeaning
Net SLSilver Lions earned in the match after costs, taken from the Total line.
RP (what the site counts)The match's CRP figure from the Total line.
Research targetsRP that went toward unlocking a whole new vehicle ("Researched unit").
Other RPRP that went to modules or research progress on a different vehicle ("Researching progress"). Kept apart so the two are never mixed.
Win / lossFrom the headline, Victory or Defeat.
Time playedFrom the match's own time information, used for the goal calculator's time estimate.

Why CRP and not the last "RP" figure?

The Total line can end with a third figure labelled RP. That figure is CRP plus the research-progress RP added together — the same match performance counted toward two things at once, not an extra payout. Using it would inflate every average and the goal calculator's estimates. The site therefore uses CRP as a match's RP.

Session IDs and duplicates

Every match has a unique Session ID. The site uses it to avoid counting a match twice:

  • Pasting overlapping ranges of your history is fine — a match seen again is struck through and left out of every total.
  • The archive keeps one copy per Session ID.
  • A match with no readable Session ID cannot be recognised as a repeat, so each such paste counts separately. If you see this, paste each match once.

What if text looks different?

Game text can change between War Thunder updates. If a paste is "not recognised", make sure it includes the headline (Victory/Defeat in the …) and the Total line. Lines that are not understood are skipped rather than breaking the analysis. See Troubleshooting.

Battle types (categories)

Each match is placed in a battle type by looking at its mode name. By default anything containing Tank Assault is its own category, and everything else is Random Battles. You can add your own rules — see The Basic view.

Duplicate warning

If some pasted matches repeat (same Session ID), a warning says how many were left out of the totals. See Reading your logs.

Battle-type categories

Open Battle-type categories to see and edit the rules that put each match in a category.

  • A rule is a keyword (looked for in the match's mode name) and a label (the category name to show).
  • By default Tank Assault has its own category; anything not matched by a rule is Random Battles.
  • To add one, type a keyword (for example Arcade) and a label (for example Arcade Battles), then Add rule. Existing matches are re-sorted next time you Analyze.
  • Rules are remembered in your browser.

Results

Overall

Four tiles: Matches (unique matches counted), Win rate, Total net SL and Total RP.

Battle type split

One row per category: matches, wins/losses, win percentage, total net SL, average SL per win and per loss, and total RP.

Goal calculator

Estimates how many matches you need to reach a goal, using your own averages.

  1. Load averages from — choose a battle type (or All Combined). Win rate, average win/loss RP and average win/loss SL are filled in from your data. You can edit any of them to ask "what if".
  2. RP goal remaining and SL goal remaining — type what you still need (either or both).
  3. +30% RP Booster — toggle if you use the booster; it multiplies expected RP by 1.3.

You get: expected RP and net SL per match, expected time per match, matches needed for each goal, and an estimated playtime. The big number is matches needed for whichever goal takes longer, labelled RP-bound, SL-bound or tied. Leave a goal empty to ignore it.

Tip: averages come from your own matches, so the estimate gets better with more data and with the right battle type chosen.

RP by research target

A table of the RP that went toward unlocking new vehicles, largest first.

Other RP

A collapsed section (click to expand) for RP that went to modules and research progress on other vehicles. It is separate on purpose.

Per-match detail

Every match in the session, one row each: result, battle type, mode, mission, net SL, RP and target. Repeated matches appear struck through with the figures blanked and the note DUPLICATE — excluded.

For very large sessions the table first shows the first 200 rows; use Show 500 more or Show all under it. This keeps the page fast on slower devices. The totals above always cover all matches, and exports and printing always include every match.

Printing and Save as PDF

Print / Save PDF uses your browser's print dialog (choose Save as PDF as the printer to make a file).

  • The printout keeps your chosen colour theme.
  • Buttons, the paste box and the "show more" row are left out.
  • Repeated (duplicate) matches are left out, just like in the totals.
  • Every match is included even if the screen only shows the first 200.
  • Text size follows a fixed print size, not your on-screen text-size setting.

If you would rather save ink, switch to a light preset such as Daylight before printing. For a file you can share, Export HTML makes a standalone report.

Session text is remembered

What you pasted is remembered in your browser, so refreshing the page does not lose it. Clear forgets it.

The Advanced view

The Advanced view reads the archive — every match you analyzed in the Basic view, saved in your browser. Open it from Advanced view →; ← Basic view returns you. The two views share the same archive and the same look.

If the archive is empty, the page says so and points you to the Basic view (or to importing a raw export).

Library

A table of every archived match.

  • Search looks in mission, category and target.
  • Battle type and Win + loss / Victories / Defeats narrow the list.
  • Sort: newest first, oldest first, most SL, most RP or longest.
  • Detail only shows only matches that have stored detail.
  • Totals above the table follow the current filters.
  • A ● marks a match with full detail; a ○ marks a summary-only match (it came from a minimal export or an HTML report, which do not contain the original text).

The table shows the first 500 rows of the current filter, with a way to show all. Searching and filtering never needs to unpack your archive, so it stays quick.

Match detail

Click a ● row to open that match's full breakdown:

  • net and earned SL, with repair, ammunition and respawn costs;
  • a per-vehicle table;
  • every scoring event — time, vehicle, weapon, target, SL and RP (hover an amount to see its base, premium-account and booster parts);
  • flat rewards, research progress and used items;
  • the original log text.

Buttons let you copy the raw text, export this match (raw), or delete it (it asks first; a deleted match is only recoverable from a raw export). Esc or Close ✕ closes the panel.

Insights

Statistics across the whole archive, in three collapsible tables:

  • By map — results per map.
  • By vehicle — time and kills per vehicle.
  • Earnings by event type — which kinds of events earn the most SL and RP.

The vehicle and event tables only use matches that have stored detail (●). They are filled in when you open them, using small summaries saved with each match, so they appear quickly even for big archives.

Where is the archive?

In your browser, on this device only (Privacy and safety). If your browser cannot use its main database, a smaller fallback of roughly 5 MB is used, and if nothing works the site keeps working for the current session without saving.

Your match archive lives only in your browser on one device. These are the ways to keep it safe and to move data around.

Importing

Use Import Report (Basic) or Import raw / log file (Advanced).

  • The Basic view accepts raw exports, minimal exports, HTML reports and plain .txt logs.
  • The Advanced view accepts raw exports and plain logs.
  • Everything is checked on import: anything that does not look like a real match is skipped (and counted), never loaded blindly.
  • Matches already in your archive are skipped, so importing twice does not duplicate anything.
  • Very large files are refused with a message (the limits are in Privacy and safety).

Moving to another device or browser

  1. On the old device: Export Raw.
  2. Move the file (cloud drive, email to yourself, USB…).
  3. On the new device: open the site, then Import Report (Basic) or Import raw / log file (Advanced).

Colour presets move separately — see Colours and text.

A good backup habit

  • Export Raw after a long session, and before clearing browser data, reinstalling or switching devices.
  • Keep a couple of dated copies (the file name already includes the date and time).
  • After restoring, open the Advanced view's Storage section to confirm the match count.

What clears your archive

Clearing your browser's "site data" or "cookies and site data" for this site; using a private/incognito window (it is discarded when closed); or Delete entire archive. Clear in the Basic view does not touch it.

Colours and text

You can make the site look the way you like, and it remembers your choice. The same look is used on both views and in exported reports.

Quick pick: eight presets

The row of swatches in the top bar switches the whole site at once:

Blue (the original default), Amber, Slate, Forest, Crimson, Violet, Teal and Daylight (a light theme). Hover a swatch for its name. The selected one is marked.

Your choice is applied before the page first appears, so reloading or switching between views does not flash the default blue.

The Customise panel

Click Customise… next to the swatches. The panel docks to the side (on a phone it covers more of the screen) and everything you change applies instantly. Close it with its close button or Esc.

Start from a preset

Pick any preset (built-in or your own) from the list as a starting point. Reset to preset undoes your edits.

Use the colour square to pick, or type a colour as #rrggbb (for example #1a2b3c). Text on buttons and banners automatically switches between dark and light so it stays readable on whatever accent or loss colour you choose.

Readability check

Each line shows how clearly one colour reads on another (a contrast ratio):

  • 4.5:1 or more — good.
  • 3 to 4.5:1 — weak, but allowed (a warning is shown).
  • Under 3:1 — refused: the new colours are not applied and the panel says which pair is too low. Your typing is kept.

Refused colours wait for you: as soon as every pair is readable, the colours apply by themselves. This is what lets you change from a dark look to a light one one colour at a time (for example background first, then text).

Font style and text size

  • Font style: monospace (the original), sans-serif or serif.
  • Text size: small, medium or large. It scales all text on the page. Printing ignores it.

Saving your own presets

In My presets, give your current colours a name and save them.

  • Names may use English or Spanish letters (including á é í ó ú ü ñ and ¿ ¡), numbers, spaces and basic punctuation, up to 30 characters. A setting allows other characters if you need them.
  • You can keep 20 presets. If you want more, the panel explains the risk and lets you go up to 100 (more saved presets means more stored data and a longer list).
  • Rename or delete any of your presets.
  • Export your presets to a file and Import them on another device. Imported files are checked strictly; bad entries are skipped.

Reset everything

Reset everything to default returns to Blue, monospace and medium text. Your saved presets are kept.

Phone, offline and slow devices

The site is built to be reliable on small screens, old devices and poor connections. This page explains what you will see and what you can do.

On a phone

Below about 680 pixels wide the top bar's controls (view switch, tutorial, GitHub link, colours) would not fit in a row, so they fold behind a Menu button.

  • Tap Menu to open the controls. The same menu lists Jump to links for the page's sections (for example Overall, Goal calculator, Per-match detail on the Basic view, or Library, Insights, Storage on the Advanced view). A section is only listed while it is on screen.
  • Tap outside the menu, press Esc, or pick an item to close it.
  • The guided tours open the menu for you when a step points at something inside it.
  • Wide screens keep the normal top bar.

Tables scroll sideways when they are wider than the screen. Tip: the Text size setting (Colours and text) can make everything easier to read.

On a slow connection

  • The whole site is small: about 60 KB of data for a first visit, and it is loaded in a handful of files rather than dozens, so there are fewer slow round trips.
  • If loading takes longer than a few seconds you will see a calm note at the bottom: Still loading — this is taking a while on your connection. The page will work in a moment. It disappears by itself.
  • If part of the page fails to arrive, a note says Part of this page didn't load — check your connection with a Reload button, instead of leaving a page that looks fine but does nothing.
  • The optional parts (colour panel, tutorials, phone menu) load after the main page, so you can start pasting and analyzing sooner.
  • Your saved colour theme is applied from the very first moment even on a slow connection.

Offline use

After your first successful visit the site saves itself in your browser (a "service worker" cache), so:

  • return visits open instantly, even on a terrible connection;
  • the whole app works with no connection at all — analyzing, the archive, the Advanced view and insights all work offline;
  • the example data works offline once it has been loaded at least once.

Updates

When a new version is published it is downloaded quietly in the background. It never swaps in by itself while you are working. A note appears — A new version is ready. — with Reload and Later.

  • Reload switches to the new version.
  • Later dismisses the note; it is offered again next visit.

If you close every tab of the site, the new version simply takes over next time you open it.

Offline limits

  • Load Example Data needs a connection the first time.
  • The very first visit needs a connection (there is nothing saved yet).
  • Opening the page directly from a file on disk skips the offline cache.
  • This documentation is not part of the offline cache.

Developers on localhost

The offline cache is off when the site is opened from localhost, so edits are never hidden behind a cache. See How it works for how to turn it on for testing.

Big sessions

With thousands of matches the page stays responsive:

  • The Basic view's match table shows the first 200 rows with Show 500 more / Show all.
  • Saving to the archive runs in the background, so the page does not freeze after Analyze.
  • The Advanced view lists matches without unpacking them, and fills its insight tables from small stored summaries.

Very large pasted logs (megabytes of text) can still make colour changes and the colour panel feel slower on weak devices, because the browser has to lay out all that text in the paste box. If that happens: Clear the box after analyzing (your archive keeps the matches).

Old browsers

The site needs a reasonably modern browser: it uses the browser's built-in compression and database features. If the page cannot start, a message with a Reload button explains that. Updating your browser fixes it.

Tips and tricks

Pasting

  • Paste a lot at once. There is no need to do one match at a time; paste a whole stretch of history.
  • Overlap is safe. Pasting the same matches again never double-counts them — repeats are matched by Session ID.
  • Paste each match once if it has no Session ID. Matches without a readable ID can't be recognised as repeats.
  • Paste, Analyze, Clear, repeat. The archive keeps every match you analyze, so you can clear the box between sessions without losing history.

Getting better numbers

  • Add rules for your game modes. Use the Battle-type categories section so Arcade, events or anything else gets its own row and its own averages for the goal calculator.
  • Pick the right battle type in the goal calculator. The averages it loads come from the type you choose; "All Combined" mixes everything.
  • Play with "what if". Edit the win rate or the average RP/SL in the goal calculator to see how a better win rate changes the number of matches needed.
  • Use the booster toggle if you play with the +30% RP booster so the estimate matches reality.
  • RP vs "Other RP". "RP by research target" is what goes toward new vehicles; module and research-progress RP is in the collapsed "Other RP" table. Both matter; they just aren't mixed.

Advanced view

  • Hover amounts in a match's detail to see the base, premium-account and booster parts of every SL and RP figure.
  • Use "Detail only" to hide summary-only matches when you want vehicle or event analysis.
  • Open the insight tables you need. Vehicle and event tables fill in when opened.
  • Sort by "Longest" or "Most SL" to find your outliers quickly.
  • Export just one match from its detail panel to share or save it.

Backups

  • Export Raw before you clear browser data, switch phones or reinstall your browser.
  • Prefer .json.gz — it's the smallest, and the same data.
  • Check the match count in the Advanced view's Storage section after a restore.

Looks

  • Try Daylight in bright sunlight or before printing.
  • Raise the text size on a small screen instead of zooming the whole browser.
  • Move a setup between devices: export your colour presets and import them on the other device.
  • Change two colours in a row to go from a dark to a light look; the site waits until the pair is readable.

Speed

  • Clear the paste box after analyzing a very large log (the archive keeps the matches).
  • Use "Show all" in the Basic table only when you need to scroll everything; the first 200 rows are usually enough.
  • Visit once while online: after that, every later visit is instant and works offline (Phone, offline and slow devices).

Keyboard

  • Esc closes the tour, the Customise panel, the phone menu and the match-detail panel.
  • ← → move through the tour steps.
  • Buttons, swatches and table rows can all be reached with Tab and activated with Enter or Space.

Troubleshooting and FAQ

"No matches recognized in that text"

The text needs to include a match headline (Victory in the […] mission! or Defeat…) and, ideally, the Total line. Copy a complete match report from War Thunder. See Reading your logs.

My totals look too low

  • Repeated matches are left out of totals — check for the duplicate warning.
  • If you only pasted part of a report, the missing parts can't be counted.
  • Check the battle-type split: matches may be in a different category than you expected.

My totals look different from the game

The site counts the match's CRP as RP (not the larger figure at the end of the Total line, which also includes research-progress RP). See Reading your logs.

The Advanced view says the archive is empty

Matches are saved when you click Analyze in the Basic view. If you used a private window, cleared site data, or opened the page from a different address (for example the stable site), the archive there is a different one. Import a raw export to restore.

Some matches have no detail (○)

They came from a minimal export or an HTML report, which hold only summaries. Only matches pasted as text, or imported from a raw export, have full detail (●).

"Load Example Data" does nothing / fails

The example is fetched from the website, so it can't load when the page is opened straight from a file on disk, or with no connection the first time. Open the hosted page, or run a small local web server.

Import said "could not read that file"

Make sure the file is an export from this tool (raw, minimal, HTML report) or a plain .txt log. Very large files (over about 150 MB) are refused. Summary-only files can't be imported in the Advanced view — use the Basic view.

My colours won't apply

One of the readability pairs is under 3:1, so the set is refused. The panel names the pair. Change a colour until every pair is readable, then the new colours apply by themselves.

My preset name was rejected

Names accept English/Spanish letters, numbers, spaces and basic punctuation, up to 30 characters. A setting lets you allow other characters.

The page looks unstyled or says "Part of this page didn't load"

A file didn't arrive (usually a poor connection). Press Reload. If it keeps happening, check your connection or try again later. After your first good visit the site is cached and loads from your device.

I see "A new version is ready"

A newer version was downloaded in the background. Reload switches to it; Later keeps the current one for now.

The site seems stuck on an old version

Use the Reload button in the update note. If there is no note, close all tabs of the site and open it again. As a last resort, clear the site's data in your browser — but export a raw backup first, because that also deletes your archive.

Everything vanished

The archive and settings live in your browser. Clearing site data, using a private window or switching browsers/devices means starting empty. Restore from a raw export (Backups, import and export).

Print / Save PDF looks wrong

  • Run Analyze first.

Privacy and safety

Your data stays with you

  • Nothing is uploaded. Pasted logs, your archive, your settings and your exports are processed and stored entirely inside your browser. The site has no account system and no server that receives your data.
  • No tracking. The pages make no network requests except to load their own files (and the optional example data).
  • You decide what leaves. Data only leaves your device when you deliberately save an export file and share it yourself.

The stable and Dev sites

The Dev and stable sites live under the same web address in your browser. They keep their archives separately, so one can't damage the other. The four original colour themes are shared as a setting, because the stable site reads the same setting.

Safe against what you paste

The site treats everything it reads — logs, files, settings — as untrusted:

  • Escaping. Names and text from logs are always shown as plain text, never run as code.
  • A strict content policy. The pages only run their own scripts, so even a mistake could not run injected code. Exported reports carry their own policy.
  • Imports are rebuilt, not trusted. Each imported match is re-created from checked fields; bad entries are skipped and counted.
  • Settings are validated. Corrupted or hand-edited saved settings fall back to defaults.
  • Unusual names are harmless. A map or vehicle called something odd such as "constructor" is just a name.

How it works

A short, plain-language tour of what is under the hood. You don't need any of this to use the site.

The big picture

The site is a set of static web pages. Your browser downloads them once and runs everything locally. There is no server doing the analysis.

  1. Reading. Pasted text is split into matches. Each match's headline, scoring events, costs, research lines, Session ID and Total line are read with careful, bounded patterns (so strange text can't make them run away).
  2. Checking. Every value is cleaned and limited before it is used.
  3. Counting. Duplicates (same Session ID) are set aside; the rest are totalled by battle type, research target and so on.
  4. Saving. The original text of each match is compressed and stored in your browser's database, along with a small summary used for fast listing and insights.
  5. Showing. Tables are built with all text escaped.

The two views

  • Basic works on what you paste (plus anything you import).
  • Advanced works on the saved archive. It lists matches from the stored summaries without unpacking anything, and only decompresses a match when you open it.

The archive

Each match is stored by its Session ID, so the same match can only exist once. Compression uses the browser's built-in gzip, done on a background worker so the page doesn't freeze. Insight tables use per-match rollups saved with each match; if the rollup format ever improves, old ones are rebuilt automatically. Summaries carry a version for the same reason.

Colours

Colours are stored as nine values and applied to the page through CSS variables. A tiny script in the page head applies your saved look before the first paint, which is why there's no flash of the default theme. Readability is measured with standard contrast ratios.

Speed and reliability features

  • Bundled files. The pages load one core file each (plus an optional "extras" file), instead of ~15 separate ones.
  • Load guard. A tiny script shows still loading or part of this page didn't load notes with a Reload button.
  • Background worker. Compression and insight rollups run off the main thread, with a fallback if workers aren't available.
  • Service worker (offline cache). After the first visit the whole app is cached under a version name. Updates wait for you to press Reload. It only ever touches caches that start with its own prefix. On localhost it's off unless you set a browser setting called enableServiceWorkerLocally to true.
  • Capped tables. The Basic match table shows 200 rows at a time; the Advanced one 500.

For people who change the site

The scripts the pages load are generated from the source files by a small build step, and tests rebuild them automatically and fail if they're stale. The project README explains the workflow, and the test suite README explains the checks (parsing, both views, storage, security, colours, theme flash, offline, build, phone menu, size-agnostic invariants and performance). The low-end investigation is in tests/results/low-end-report.md.

Behaviour worth knowing

  • Refused colours wait instead of blocking you. Edits are drafted: if the current set is unreadable (< 3:1) it is not applied and the panel says which pair is too low, but the boxes keep what you typed. As soon as every pair passes, the colours apply and save by themselves. This is what lets you turn a dark look into a light one one colour at a time, since every intermediate step is unreadable.
  • Text on buttons and banners flips between dark and light automatically so it stays readable on whatever accent / loss colour is chosen.
  • Text size scales all text (CSS sizes are in rem); printing ignores it.
  • The four original presets keep their exact colours, so a few of their dim-text pairs are in the "weak" band (e.g. Blue's dim text on panel 2 is 3.99:1). The new presets are all ≥ 4.5:1.

Loading without a flash

The saved look is applied by appearance-core.js, loaded in the <head> before the stylesheet, so the colours, font and text size are set before anything can be painted (and the page's 0.15 s colour transition has nothing to animate). Without it the look was applied by themes.js, which loads at the very end of the page, so the default Blue could show first and fade into the saved look. The redirect page (index.html) uses the same file. tests/suites/flash.js checks this by slowing themes.js down and inspecting the colours when the page body first appears and on every frame after.
